# Cemetery rules

- Grave form encodes state, nothing else (R-UI-1, version 0.5): a rounded stone with a party hat and flowers for Alive, a tilted stone with a hand clawing out of the dirt for Restless, a cross for Haunted, a cracked tilted stone with a lounging skeleton in a onesie for Dead, an open grave with a dancing devil-costumed zombie and a pink glow for Possessed. Colour follows state. Every grave of one State looks the same; only the animation phase and the Dancer schedule differ.
- A ghost above a grave means one or more advisories. Nothing else is a ghost.
- No continuous visual encodings. No tombstone height, no opacity scales. Categorical only. The three depth Tiers (scene/tiers.ts) are categorical: Possessed and Dead in front at scale 1, Haunted and Restless in the middle at 0.8, Alive at the back at 0.64. A grave's Tier follows its scan-date State, rows are centred and spread up to 1.07, 1.334 and 1.667 cell widths apart to fill the width (205 px in every Tier, so the middle and back rows line up with the front columns), triage order holds within a Tier and in the keyboard order, and grave rectangles never overlap. When the graves fit, the sky grows so they stand at the bottom above the Footer bar. A replay's one grave is drawn full size, at up to 1.8.
- Order graves by triage: Possessed first, then state from least healthy to most healthy (Dead, Haunted, Restless, Alive), then advisory severity, then transitive reach, then name.
- Readable without the legend. If a first-time viewer cannot tell which grave to fix first, the encoding is wrong.
- WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. Every grave reachable by keyboard. prefers-reduced-motion respected.
- 300 graves without visible lag. Canvas or WebGL for the scene, DOM for the drawer.
- The scene is a 2D canvas (scene/Scene.tsx). Grave buttons in overlay/GraveButtons.tsx give keyboard focus and aria-labels.
- Web imports only types from @sexton/api.
- Web fetches only addresses that pass checkScanUrl in load/use-scan-loader.ts: a scan file on the page's own origin (the demo is /demo/scan.json) or the API base from load/api-base.ts. The API base is a same-origin path such as /api or an https URL.
- The drawer opens a pull request with POST to the API base plus /remediate. The body is { repo, package }. Show the button only for a hosted scan whose response has actions.remediate true.
- The site footer links the evidence pack as JSON and Markdown downloads ("Evidence JSON", "Evidence Markdown"), beside the scan time and the policy line.
- ESLint bans dangerouslySetInnerHTML, innerHTML, outerHTML, insertAdjacentHTML and node: imports in packages/web/src. Render scan text as text only.
- Scenery is seeded (scene/scenery-seed.ts). No Math.random. Stars and grass depend on the scene width only, never on the scan.
- The Shadow under a grave and the Stone texture are decoration, the same for every grave of a State. They use fixed alpha bytes from scene/palette.ts. No opacity scales.
- The PR preview parses with drawer/markdown.ts, which handles exactly what the engine's pr-body.ts emits. A link is made only for an https:// address, with target="_blank" and rel="noopener noreferrer". Output is React elements only.
- The Result area (the outcome of opening a pull request) sits outside the scrolling drawer body, so it stays in view.
- The drawer (drawer/Drawer.tsx, spec clean-site) slides in from the right, 680 px wide and full width under 700 px, with the breadcrumb "← Cemetery / <name>", the name, version, State badge and scan date, and the tabs Evidence, The fix and History (role tablist, arrow keys). The fix tab comes from drawer/fix-view.ts and shows at most one copy button; a copy goes through drawer/clipboard.ts. Escape closes an open Pop-over before the drawer, and every close returns focus to the grave button or List row that opened it.
- The header is not inert while the drawer is open. The scene, the headline and chips, the Footer bar and the Panel are. A press on the Scan field closes the drawer and focuses the field.
- The scene is full-bleed: no horizontal padding on main.
- At 360 px there is no horizontal scroll, with the drawer closed, open or the legend open. Below 700 px the drawer is full width.
- Every colour is a token in scene/palette.ts. A new token joins the contrast tests. styles.css holds no colour literal. The palette is night-only.
- The scan time is shown in a <time> element with a machine-readable dateTime.
- Grass is short seeded tufts along the row lines and one line near the bottom of the ground (scene/scenery-seed.ts, drawn by drawTufts in scene/draw.ts). Tufts depend on the scene width only.
- The ground reaches the bottom of the viewport. hooks/use-free-height.ts gives the free height and planScene takes it as minHeight. Grave positions do not change.
- The scrubber (time/Scrubber.tsx) is a native range input named "History date" with aria-valuetext. Its stops come from time/stops.ts: the start, one stop for each change of state, ghost or version, and the scan date. It is not rendered when the response has no timelines.
- Web holds no state rule. The Engine writes Segments into the scan response (timelines). time/form-at.ts looks up the Segment at the chosen instant. Never copy a state rule into web.
- Triage order and grave positions always use the scan date. Only form, colour, ghost and the inscription version follow the chosen date.
- The drawer always shows the scan date. A note under the scrubber says so when an earlier date is chosen.
- Two forms are added and neither changes the grave form of a State. The Not-published form is an unmarked mound with no ghost, for a package whose installed version is not yet published on the chosen date. The pull request marker is a pennant beside the grave, and the grave button name ends in "pull request opened". It is not a ghost. Both have a legend chip.
- The marker comes from the scan response (pullRequests, read from the remediation log) and from pull requests opened in this session.
- The burial plays once for each package and loaded scan after a pull request is opened, when the drawer closes or at once if it is closed. It lasts 1200 ms in three fixed phases: the stone sinks, a mound shows, then the stone returns with the pennant. Under prefers-reduced-motion there is no burial animation: the Vigil plays in its place, the Priest standing still beside the grave for 1200 ms with no stone motion. The burial never changes the state, shape or fill of a grave.
- A replay is a scan response loaded from /replay/axios.json or /replay/keyv.json on the page origin. "Watch a real attack" in the Footer bar opens the attack picker, a Pop-over with one button per replay. The addresses ?replay=axios and ?replay=keyv load them. Any other replay value loads nothing.
- A replay opens at its first Stop. Every other scan opens at its scan date.
- The Play control (time/Scrubber.tsx, time/play.ts, time/use-play.ts) is a native button in a replay's Footer bar before the History label, the date and the slider, shown only for more than one Stop. Home has no Play control. Its name is "Play" or "Pause". It moves one Stop every 1,500 ms and stops at the last Stop. Play at the last Stop restarts at the first. The slider, Back to now and a new load pause it. Each start, step, end and pause is announced in a polite live line, never role="status". It never starts on its own.
- When the response has timelinesOmitted "too-large", the line "History is not shown for this scan because it is too large." takes the place of the scrubber.
- The shell (spec clean-site): a one-row header of at most 64 px (the wordmark link, the scan tag, the Scan field with Scan, then "What is Sexton?", Legend and Tour); the Welcome bar in the page flow; Home with the headline, the chips and the Graveyard / List switch, the Scene card, the Footer bar (footer.foot-bar: the History label, the slider with its start label and "Today, <scan date>", Back to now at an earlier date, or the omitted history line, and "Watch a real attack") and the Panel (the Inspector and the Fix order, 372 px on the right from 1100 px, under the Scene below it); then the site footer. The screen state (load/screen-state.ts, held by load/use-view.ts) is the one owner of the open View owner and Pop-over. The help line and the date badge sit in the Scene's sky band (scene/overlay-band.ts), clear of every Art box. A filter draws the other graves at one fixed dim alpha (DIM_ALPHA_BYTE) and takes their buttons out of the tab order; the grave under the pointer gets the hover ring in ringHover on the Ring casing.
- The palette is the night of cemetery-undead: Alive #8fe3d0, Restless #e69f00, Haunted #56b4e9, Dead #bfb8ad, Possessed #cc79a7 on a violet sky with an orange harvest moon. The five state colours stay at least 8 apart in CIEDE2000 under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (test/colour-vision.test.ts). The Lantern glow and the Possessed glow are Glow stops in the contrast tests, and a grave has at most one glow. The Halo counts as part of the Possessed glow: the Lantern grave shows the Lantern glow alone.
- Fonts are self-hosted under public/fonts as woff2 files with their OFL files: Inter for the interface and the canvas grave name labels (Inter 600), Cormorant Garamond 600 for the wordmark and headlines. Versions, signal keys and diffs use the system monospace stack. No other font ships, and no font request leaves the page origin.
- Characters are original vector art (scene/characters/vector.ts) at 12 Poses a second, driven by one clock in Scene.tsx that only repaints the canvas and stops while the page is hidden. Under prefers-reduced-motion every Character holds one still Pose and the bats hold still. A Dead skeleton dances 2 s in every 12 s for one Dead grave in five (at least one when any Dead grave exists), chosen from a seed of the package name. The ghost is original art; never draw or embed the Kiro logo.
- The backdrop (church, tree, fence, bats, fog, vignette, grain, the cratered moon) depends on the width only and is drawn under every grave. The hovered or focused grave gets the Lantern glow.
- A replay screen shows the banner text "Reconstructed from public reports. Not a live scan." (replay/ReplayBanner.tsx) in a card over the scene, top left, with the chosen Stop's date; the header keeps its normal height. A source link is made only for an https:// address.
- No grave covers another grave's name (spec cemetery-undead-2). Everything a grave draws but its glow lies in its Art box (scene/art-box.ts): inside its own cell's vertical band and at most 40 cell units past either side. The bounds probe of test/art-box.test.ts draws every Form, Pose, Ghost drift, Burst and Priest phase and fails on a mark outside its box.
- The Halo (scene/halo.ts) is the strong part of the Possessed glow, at HALO_ALPHA_BYTE. It is drawn only where no checked token can sit: every grave's Part, Ghost and pennant boxes and name label, the Art box of a grave whose Burst plays, and the burial regions of a grave whose burial or Vigil plays are cut out of it, each with a soft edge (HALO_GROW 12, HALO_BLUR 3). Part boxes are 4-unit grid blocks measured by the outline probe; the Priest is cut by his own PRIEST_PARTS. The burial regions follow the sinking stone, in BURIAL_CUT_STEPS steps of the phase, so no column stays where the stone was. The selection ring and the focus outline are not cut: each sits on its Ring casing, an ink line on both sides of the gold, so the gold never sits on the Halo. haloMask is the one source of the cuts for the painter and the test. The existing glow contrast check stays unedited, and the ring and focus colours are each at least 3:1 against ink.
- The pit edge of the open grave is costume.flower, at least 20 CIEDE2000 from the ring gold. The ring is the only gold outline near a grave.
- A replay's card over the scene shows the chosen Stop's date; the site footer keeps the replay's window end in a <time> element. Its story card lists its sources on one line after "Sources:".
- The replay Burst plays when a replay arrives at its Burst Stop, the first Stop at which its grave is Possessed, found from the Segments: the lid flies, seeded dirt and shards fly, the zombie rises with the Rise clip. It has no caption. It plays again on each arrival. Under Reduced motion the final pose shows at once.
- During a burial the Priest (scene/characters/priest.ts), original art, walks to the grave and reads.
- The Load moment raises the graves out of the ground in triage order on the first scan of a page load, within 1,000 ms, once per page load, and never under Reduced motion. The grave rectangles never move.
- A still grave body, a ghost and a glow are drawn from offscreen sprites (scene/sprites.ts), painted once per shape, fill, Pose and size, so 300 graves in the front row repaint inside 16 ms. Only a real canvas uses them; the tests record the direct drawing that both share.
